Privacy is becoming a condition for crypto adoption, and UR puts Bittensor in the network layer

Crypto now carries enough ordinary economic activity that a public ledger has become a commercial barrier.

Encryption hides what was sent, but the connection still shows who sent it. SN25 turns that missing layer into a commodity that anyone can supply and the network prices in TAO.

What could break it

New enforcement against privacy developers, and fees that accrue to issuers and applications rather than network tokens.

$310B of stablecoins now sits on ledgers anyone can read

Evidence

Multicoin reported more than $310 billion in stablecoin supply and $121 billion managed by DeFi protocols in February 2026. a16z counted over $175 billion in Bitcoin and Ether exchange-traded products in October 2025.

What it means

Anyone who links an address to a company can read its payroll, supplier terms and positions. At this scale confidentiality becomes an operating requirement, which favours infrastructure that verifies transactions while limiting who can read them.

Qualification

These figures measure capital, not activity that needs privacy. Much stablecoin supply sits on exchanges, so the share that requires confidentiality is smaller and not yet measured.

A month of onchain payments shows a competitor a company’s payroll, suppliers and treasury moves

What controlled disclosure means

The network still verifies every payment, but amounts and counterparties are readable only by the company, its auditor and regulators.

What it means

Businesses do not need secrecy, they need to choose who can read their records. Without that, putting payroll and treasury onchain publishes them.

US policy has moved from sanctioning a privacy tool to legislating for stablecoins

Also in 2025, dates to confirm

Paxos and Aleo announce USAD, a private stablecoin. The Ethereum Foundation forms a dedicated privacy team.

What it means

The legal risk that kept institutions away has narrowed, and demand now comes from issuers, payroll providers and protocol teams.

Qualification

The ruling turned on whether immutable contracts are property. Developer liability remains open after a 2025 conviction.

Zcash’s rerating shows investors will pay for privacy, yet only about a quarter of ZEC is shielded

Evidence

ZEC rose from about $50 in September 2025 to over $350 by late October, and Coin Metrics put it up nearly tenfold since October by mid-November. Shielded holdings reached about 4.9M ZEC.

What it means

Investors will pay for privacy, but most ZEC and most activity still sit on the transparent side: about $92,000 of $110,000 in Q2 2026 fee revenue came from transparent transfers. A private asset still leaks at its entry and exit points.

For UR

That leak sits at the connection, which is the layer SN25 supplies.

SN25 turns private bandwidth into a digital commodity that validators measure and TAO pays for

What the design provides

Reliable capacity and geographic coverage that many privacy operators can draw on, paid for through an incentive that rewards measured performance rather than claims.

What UR does not claim

UR does not claim protection against global traffic correlation and does not use mixing or cover traffic, so its positioning has to match these guarantees.

Why it matters

Network privacy is a separate problem from transaction privacy. Routing capacity of this kind complements mixnets such as Nym rather than replacing them.

UR sits in the weakest value-capture position today and has to move from emissions to operator fees

Computation fees

Applications and enterprises pay for encrypted operations, proofs and secure computation. Zama, Nillion, Seismic, Nockchain.

Transaction fees

Users pay per private transfer, shielding or withdrawal; pools compete on fees. Railgun, Zcash, Aleo.

Issuer economics

Reserve yield accrues to issuers and distributors, little reaches network tokens. Paxos USAD; Bron payroll.

Implication for UR

The case depends on operators paying for UR’s capacity in fees before subnet emissions decline, which would move it towards the transaction or computation models.

Regulation and token value capture are the two risks most likely to break the thesis

Regulation

New enforcement against non-custodial privacy developers in the US or EU, after a 2025 conviction left developer liability unresolved.

Token value capture

Usage grows while protocol fee revenue stays flat, because fees accrue to issuers and applications.

Hardware and MPC trust

A material enclave exploit or collusion event at a major network.

Cost of FHE and ZK

Proving and FHE costs stop falling, or hardware acceleration is delayed.

Permissioned alternatives

Institutional stablecoin volume concentrates on permissioned ledgers.

UR’s network guarantees

Privacy operators require mixing or cover traffic that UR does not offer.

How it works

Bitcoin miners compete on hash puzzles with no use outside the network. Nockchain miners instead compete to produce zero-knowledge proofs, so the work that secures the chain is the kind of computation applications could pay for. A proof lets a party verify a computation without seeing its inputs, which extends privacy into financial services and confidential computing.

Investment thesis

Investor commentary places NOCK alongside alternative stores of value, new proof-of-work coins and privacy coins, combining Bitcoin’s monetary properties with programmable public and private applications.

What has to be true

Demand emerges for the proofs miners produce, beyond the work needed to secure the chain. Proof generation on Nockchain is cost-competitive with dedicated proving services. Enterprise users accept a proof-of-work network for confidential computation.

Open questions

The same commentary linked NOCK’s October 2025 price rise to the Zcash rally, so recent performance may reflect sentiment toward privacy coins more than usage. Funding details are as reported by the company and investors.

Privacy should be built into the network, supplied by anyone and paid for by the people who use it

Private by default

Payments, queries and connections should reveal only what their owners choose to share. Disclosure should be something a user decides to make, with an auditor or a regulator, and never a side effect of using the network.

Open supply

Anyone with bandwidth, hardware or code should be able to add capacity from wherever they are. A network that depends on one operator, or one country, can be switched off.

Measured, then paid

Validators should measure what each provider actually delivers, and rewards should follow that measurement. Over time those rewards should come from customers rather than from emissions.

Bittensor showed that an open network can produce intelligence and price it as a commodity. Privacy is the next commodity that belongs on such a network, and SN25 is where it can be supplied, measured and paid for.
