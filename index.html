Executive summary

The privacy layer that hides who you are is unpriced, and UR is built for it

The problem
$306B
Stablecoins on public ledgers

More than crypto ETFs, DeFi and tokenised assets combined. Every payment is visible.

The proof
$20.8B
Zcash market cap

A US Zcash ETF holds $463M, and the first confidential payroll and OTC trades ran in 2026.

The opportunity
~$46M
All five network-privacy tokens combined

Encryption hides the payment but not the sender: in tests, a network observer linked over 95% of wallet users to their IP address from timing alone.

The betUR on Bittensor SN25
What it isUR is the Bittensor subnet that pays people to route private traffic for URnetwork, whose VPN has about 350K monthly users (team-reported).
What would prove itThe proof is the first operator buying UR to deposit against real customer traffic, a link built into the design but set to zero for now.
What would break itA conviction when Tornado Cash developer Roman Storm is retried in April 2027 would raise the legal risk for privacy developers, and the other threat is stakers pulling TAO from SN25 before deposits begin.

At this scale confidentiality stops being a preference and becomes an operating requirement.

6 / 23

AI makes public ledgers easy to read and private data worth more

Both make it more expensive to operate in public.

“For users that prioritize privacy, this could become a ‘must-have’ feature.”Zach Pandl, Head of Research, Grayscale, September 2026

His point: AI could push up demand for financial privacy.

Force one

Exposure gets cheaper

Models parse transaction histories at a scale that once took analysts weeks, so watching a public ledger costs close to nothing.

Force two

Private data gets more valuable

Useful assistants need email, records and preferences to work, so people and companies disclose more to them.

Buterin and Durov both treat privacy as something you do not trade away

One runs a blockchain, the other a messaging app with over a billion users.

Vitalik Buterin
Co-founder
Ethereum
“Privacy is not about standing apart. It’s about standing together.”Why I support privacy, 14 April 2025
Why it matters
Control over information is power, so privacy protects decentralisation, personal freedom and institutions such as the secret ballot.
Where it should live
In the protocol: payments, application activity, chain reads and network-level anonymity.
The gap in practice
A roadmap. Adoption depends on wallets and applications shipping it.
Same conclusion
Pavel Durov
Founder and CEO
Telegram, over 1B monthly users
“Telegram would rather exit a market than undermine encryption with backdoors and violate basic human rights.”Telegram channel, 21 April 2025
Why it matters
Encryption protects everyday communication and personal safety, and access built for authorities also opens a door for attackers.
Where it should live
In the product, with no backdoors, even at the cost of leaving a market.
The gap in practice
Telegram’s default Cloud Chats are not end-to-end encrypted; only Secret Chats are.
Our readPrivacy has to be designed into wallets, chains and messaging from the start, which favours infrastructure over standalone privacy tools.

Crossings leak: the swap route into private Zcash

Connections leak: wallet users a passive observer identified
