Executive findings

Privacy is becoming a condition for the next phase of crypto adoption

Crypto now carries enough ordinary economic activity that public-by-default ledgers have become a commercial barrier.

The infrastructure that makes confidentiality usable is positioned to capture the next wave of business adoption.

The main uncertainty

Most confidential applications are early, and the deployments cited here are company-reported rather than independently verified.

  • Adoption has reached the scale where exposure is costly. Multicoin reported more than and in February 2026, and payroll, treasury and supplier flows now move on ledgers that publish them.See the chart
  • Businesses need controlled disclosure rather than secrecy. The requirement is a network that verifies every transaction while limiting who can read salaries, supplier terms and trading positions.See the argument
  • AI raises both the cost of transparency and the value of private data. Better analytical tools make public histories easier to read, while useful assistants need private records, which creates demand for private computation.See the argument
  • Early commercial use cases are concrete. Zama and Bron report , and Zama reports a confidential OTC transaction involving GSR.Compare projects
  • Value will accrue to privacy that customers use repeatedly. The strongest opportunities attach a clear guarantee to payments, trading or computation that customers already pay for.See the view

Onchain finance at scale

Onchain finance is now large enough that public-by-default ledgers carry a commercial cost

Capital in selected onchain financial products, $B

Latest reported value; reporting dates differ by series. Hover a figure for its source.
Evidence

Multicoin reported more than $310 billion in stablecoin supply and $121 billion managed by DeFi protocols in February 2026. a16z counted over $175 billion in Bitcoin and Ether exchange-traded products in October 2025.

What it means

Anyone who links an address to a company can read its payroll, supplier terms and positions. At this scale confidentiality becomes an operating requirement, which favours infrastructure that verifies transactions while limiting who can read them.

Qualification

These figures measure capital, not activity that needs privacy. Much stablecoin supply sits on exchanges, so the share that requires confidentiality is smaller and not yet measured.

Two founders’ theses

Buterin and Durov argue for privacy from opposite ends, and both point to demand for infrastructure

Buterin treats privacy as a structural property of decentralisation. Control over information creates power, so centralised data collection is as much a concern as centralised infrastructure. His Ethereum roadmap extends privacy across payments, application activity, blockchain queries and network anonymity, including wallet integration of Railgun and Privacy Pools.

Durov argues from the user’s side. He frames encryption as a safeguard for everyday communication and personal safety, and warns that access mechanisms built for authorities also expose users to hackers. He has said Telegram would rather leave a market than introduce encryption backdoors, while Telegram itself separates client-server encrypted Cloud Chats from end-to-end encrypted Secret Chats.

The two positions converge on one commercial point. Privacy has to be designed into the architecture rather than added later, which favours protocols and infrastructure that make confidentiality the default rather than an optional mode.

Where they differ

Buterin focuses on protocol-level privacy that any application can inherit. Durov focuses on product decisions and the refusal of backdoors.

Investor relevance

Both theses point to privacy as a default, which rewards infrastructure embedded in wallets, chains and messaging over standalone privacy tools.

Caveat

Telegram’s default Cloud Chats are not end-to-end encrypted, which shows how product convenience can limit privacy in practice.

Sources: Vitalik Buterin, Why I support privacy (Apr 2025); Vitalik Buterin, A maximally simple L1 privacy roadmap; Pavel Durov, statements on encryption and backdoors; Telegram, Cloud Chats and Secret Chats.

How it works

Bitcoin miners compete on hash puzzles with no use outside the network. Nockchain miners instead compete to produce zero-knowledge proofs, so the work that secures the chain is the kind of computation applications could pay for. A proof lets a party verify a computation without seeing its inputs, which extends privacy into financial services and confidential computing.

Investment thesis

Investor commentary places NOCK alongside alternative stores of value, new proof-of-work coins and privacy coins, combining Bitcoin’s monetary properties with programmable public and private applications.

What has to be true
  • Demand emerges for the proofs miners produce, beyond the work needed to secure the chain.Not yet evidencedEvidence that would count: paying third-party proof requests, reported onchain or by customers.
  • Proof generation on Nockchain is cost-competitive with dedicated proving services.Not yet evidencedEvidence that would count: published cost per proof against dedicated provers on a like-for-like workload.
  • Enterprise users accept a proof-of-work network for confidential computation.Not yet evidencedEvidence that would count: a named enterprise pilot or contract.
Open questions

The same commentary linked NOCK’s October 2025 price rise to the Zcash rally, so recent performance may reflect sentiment toward privacy coins more than usage. Funding details are as reported by the company and investors.

Where value accrues

Value accrues where customers pay for private computation, and least where emissions fund capacity

Computation fees. Applications and enterprises pay for encrypted operations, proofs and secure computation. Zama, Nillion, Seismic, Nockchain.
Transaction fees. Users pay per private transfer, shielding or withdrawal; pools compete on fees. Railgun, Zcash, Aleo.
Issuer economics. Reserve yield accrues to issuers and distributors, little reaches network tokens. Paxos USAD; Bron payroll.
Emissions subsidy. Token holders fund bandwidth and routing capacity through dilution before demand arrives. UR (SN25), Nym.
Implication for UR

The case depends on operators paying for UR’s capacity in fees before subnet emissions decline, which would move it towards the transaction or computation models.

Note: Khala Research classification; positions are qualitative judgements of who pays and where value accrues, not forecasts. Fee mechanics vary by project. Sources: project documentation; Multicoin Capital; a16z crypto; Paxos and Aleo announcement.

From enforcement to infrastructure

In three years privacy moved from an enforcement target to a feature that regulated institutions are building

Also in 2025, dates to confirm

Paxos and Aleo announce USAD, a private stablecoin. The Ethereum Foundation forms a dedicated privacy team.

What it means

The legal risk that kept institutions away has narrowed, and demand now comes from issuers, payroll providers and protocol teams.

Qualification

The ruling turned on whether immutable contracts are property. Developer liability remains open after a 2025 conviction.

      • Print. Download as PDF: one slide per A4 landscape page, fitted to the page.
      • Fit. Split a page rather than shrink it. Minimums are 7.5 pt for sources, 8 pt for tables and 9 pt for commentary.
