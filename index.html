$306B of stablecoins now sits on ledgers anyone can read

Stablecoins are the largest pool of onchain capital, larger than US crypto ETFs, DeFi and tokenised real-world assets put together, and every transfer between them is public.

Capital in selected onchain financial products, USD. ETF figures are net assets; tokenised assets are distributed value. Hover any segment for the detail.

Anyone who links an address to a company can read its payroll, supplier terms and positions.

Tether and Circle issue most of the supply, so a handful of contracts now carry a large share of business payments.

Businesses using stablecoins at this scale need confidentiality to protect their records.

One month onchain hands a competitor your payroll, suppliers and treasury

A fictional company pays its staff, a supplier and an exchange in stablecoins. One linked address is all a competitor needs.

Businesses need to control access to their records while allowing disclosure to auditors and regulators.

With controlled disclosure the network still verifies every payment, while amounts and counterparties stay readable only to the company, its auditor and regulators.

AI makes a public ledger cheap to read and private data more valuable

AI lowers the cost of analysing public transactions while increasing demand for access to private data.

“For users that prioritize privacy, this could become a ‘must have’ feature.”Zach Pandl, Head of Research, Grayscale, September 2026

Pandl's argument is that AI could raise demand for financial privacy, the same mechanism this slide traces.

Force one

Exposure gets cheaper

Models parse transaction histories at a scale that once took analysts weeks, so watching a public ledger costs close to nothing.

Force two

Private data gets more valuable

Useful assistants need email, records and preferences to work, so people and companies disclose more to them.

Privacy breaks at the edges, where 95% of wallet users can be unmasked by timing alone

Encryption protects what a transaction contains. It leaks where value crosses between systems and where a wallet connects to the network.

Crossings leak: the swap route into private Zcash

Connections leak: wallet users a passive observer identified

Wang et al., arXiv preprint, experiments March to April 2025. Bitcoin and Solana rows ran on testnets. The authors name Tor or VPNs as possible defences.

The market pays 450 times more to hide what moved than who moved it

Network privacy tokens trade at a fraction of one percent of transaction privacy valuations.

The five network-layer privacy tokens we track are worth about $46M combined.

The two capacity networks with larger valuations, Grass and Helium, sell to outside customers who pay for bandwidth or coverage. That suggests the network layer is valued once an outside buyer pays.

UR sells routing capacity and coverage; mixnets offer stronger anonymity

UR offers capacity across many exit addresses, but token issuance still funds its supply.

Nym is the closest rival, with mixing for stronger anonymity and a VPN of its own, so UR complements mixnets rather than replacing them.

Earlier token-paid networks remained dependent on issuance. Grass and Helium gained paying customers for their capacity.

UR holders have no claim on revenue, so value reaches SN25 through TAO inflows first and operator deposits later
